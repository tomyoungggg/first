Whop Indirect Tax Policy

1. Overview

Whop operates a global software marketplace where creators monetize and deliver digital products and services. This document establishes the framework by which Whop manages its indirect tax obligations—including sales tax, value-added tax (VAT), and goods and services tax (GST)—in the United States and foreign jurisdictions. Adherence to this policy ensures compliance, mitigates audit risk, and maintains consumer trust.

2. Terms of Service and Allocation of Responsibility

Whop's Terms of Service clearly establish that sellers using the platform are the merchants of record for the transactions they facilitate. As such, the primary obligation to collect and remit sales and use tax falls upon each seller, except where Whop is legally required to act as a "marketplace facilitator" under state or foreign law.

  • Whop's Role: Whop provides infrastructure, tools, and guidance to help sellers understand and address their tax obligations. In jurisdictions where marketplace facilitator rules apply, Whop may be required to collect and remit tax on behalf of sellers.
  • Seller's Responsibility: Sellers are responsible for understanding their own nexus, registering in applicable jurisdictions, and correctly handling exempt transactions or product-specific rules not covered by Whop's automated systems.

3. US Sales and Use Tax

3.1 Nexus Determination

Whop evaluates its physical and economic nexus in each US state on an ongoing basis. Economic nexus is monitored against each state's sales threshold (commonly $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions). States with no sales tax—Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire, and Oregon—are excluded from compliance requirements.

3.2 Registration and Compliance

Upon exceeding thresholds or establishing nexus, Whop registers for sales tax permits in the applicable states. Whop currently maintains registrations in select states and is working with a compliance partner to evaluate and expand registration coverage as needed.

3.3 Tax Collection

  • Digital Products: Whop primarily sells digital goods (e.g., software access, memberships, digital content). Taxability of digital products varies by state, and Whop applies appropriate tax rules using a third-party tax engine where it is the collecting party.
  • Rate Determination: Tax rates are determined based on the buyer's shipping or billing address using integrated tax calculation services.
  • Exemptions: Whop honors valid exemption certificates provided by customers (e.g., resellers, nonprofits) where applicable and maintains records accordingly.

3.4 Remittance and Filing

Whop remits collected taxes and files returns on the schedule determined by each state (monthly, quarterly, or annually). Filing is handled through automated compliance tools with reconciliation and oversight by the Finance team.

4. Foreign Indirect Tax

4.1 VAT/GST Obligations

Whop is registered for VAT in the UK and the EU and collects VAT on applicable sales to consumers in those jurisdictions. Further international registrations are evaluated as sales volumes and regulatory requirements evolve.

4.2 Non-Resident Registration

Where required by local law (e.g., the EU's One Stop Shop scheme), Whop registers as a non-resident vendor and submits periodic filings. Sellers transacting through Whop in foreign jurisdictions may have additional obligations depending on their business structure and activities.

5. Compliance Infrastructure and Controls

  • Tax Engine Integration: Whop integrates with a third-party tax calculation provider to determine applicable rates and taxability.
  • Record Retention: All tax-related records, including invoices, exemption certificates, and filing documentation, are retained for a minimum of seven years.
  • Audit Preparedness: Whop maintains documentation sufficient to respond to inquiries from tax authorities, including detailed transaction logs and exemption records.
  • Internal Review: The Finance team reviews tax filings and reconciliations on a regular cadence to ensure accuracy and timely remittance.

6. Ongoing Review and Change Management

This policy is reviewed at least annually and updated as necessary to reflect changes in law, business operations, or guidance from tax advisors. Significant legislative or operational changes (e.g., expansion into new product lines or markets) trigger interim policy reviews.
