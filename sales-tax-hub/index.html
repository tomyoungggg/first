Whop Indirect Tax Policy

In collaboration with its tax advisor, Deloitte Tax LLP, Whop has prepared the following Indirect Tax Policy document to guide its approach to indirect taxes.

See Taxes section in Seller Terms

1. Overview

Whop's indirect tax obligations are determined on a transaction-by-transaction basis and depend on (i) Whop's role in the applicable transaction and (ii) the applicability of marketplace facilitator, deemed supplier, or similar indirect tax laws in each jurisdiction. Whop structures its operations and transaction flows to comply with applicable indirect tax laws and to allocate collection and remittance responsibilities based on its role in each transaction, except where statutory requirements impose such obligations on Whop.

Generally:

  • Whop is responsible for collection and remittance of tax on transactions that occur through the Discover surface, with those transactions considered marketplace sales in applicable jurisdictions
  • Sellers are responsible for collection and remittance of tax on transactions that occur through direct-to-consumer payment links or embedded checkout
  • Whop may be responsible for collecting indirect taxes on sales of its services in certain jurisdictions

2. Terms of Service and Allocation of Responsibility

As stated in Whop's Terms of Service, sellers using the Whop platform are responsible for their own tax obligations. Specifically, sellers are responsible for:

  • Determining which taxes or fees apply to the sale of their products and services, or to payments made or received in connection with their use of Whop's services; and
  • Assessing, collecting, reporting, and remitting all applicable taxes on products and services sold by the seller, including but not limited to sales tax, value-added tax (VAT), income tax, and other statutory taxes.

Where required by applicable law, Whop may withhold, collect, and remit taxes on a seller's behalf to the appropriate tax authorities. Notwithstanding any such collection by Whop, sellers remain ultimately responsible for the verification, reporting, and compliance with all applicable tax obligations.

3. US Sales and Use Tax

In the United States, Whop determines its indirect tax obligations under a marketplace facilitator–based framework. Under this framework, Whop is responsible for the collection and remittance of US indirect taxes only when all of the following conditions are met:

  • Whop is acting as a marketplace facilitator with respect to the transaction;
  • The transaction constitutes a qualifying marketplace sale under applicable law;
  • Whop has established nexus in the applicable jurisdiction; and
  • The underlying product or service is taxable in that jurisdiction.

For purposes of this policy, a transaction is treated as a marketplace sale only when the transaction occurs through Whop's Discover platform, where the seller lists the product or service within the Discover marketplace and the sale is facilitated through that marketplace environment.

Sellers are responsible for indirect US tax on transactions initiated outside the Discover platform, including seller-generated payment links used in direct-to-consumer transactions or embedded checkout, which are not treated as marketplace sales.

4. Foreign Indirect Tax

In non-U.S. jurisdictions, Whop applies the same role-based indirect tax framework to determine its tax obligations, subject to jurisdiction-specific marketplace, deemed supplier, and indirect tax laws. Under this framework, Whop is responsible for the collection and remittance of indirect taxes only when all of the following conditions are met:

  • Whop is acting in a role that gives rise to indirect tax collection obligations under applicable law (including as a marketplace facilitator or deemed supplier);
  • The transaction constitutes a qualifying marketplace sale under applicable law;
  • Whop has established any required registration in the applicable jurisdiction; and
  • The underlying product or service is taxable in that jurisdiction.

For purposes of this policy, and subject to jurisdiction-specific requirements, a transaction is treated as a marketplace sale only when the transaction occurs through Whop's Discover platform, where the seller lists the product or service within the Discover marketplace and the sale is facilitated through that marketplace environment.

Sellers are responsible for indirect tax on transactions initiated outside the Discover platform, including seller-generated payment links used in direct-to-consumer transactions and embedded checkout, which are not treated as marketplace sales.

Whop currently maintains indirect tax registrations in certain non-U.S. jurisdictions, including the European Union and the United Kingdom. Under the current operating model in these jurisdictions, Whop generally collects and remits indirect tax on all transactions processed through the platform unless a seller provides an explicit written request to assume responsibility where permitted by law.

Foreign indirect tax collection and remittance are administered through third-party service providers, Stripe and Taxually. Tax invoices issued for these transactions identify Whop as the party handling indirect tax.

5. Compliance Infrastructure and Controls

Whop has engaged Numeral as its core indirect tax compliance platform to support both Whop's own tax obligations and seller-managed tax compliance, depending on the structure of each transaction and the allocation of responsibility under applicable law.

Whop-Managed Tax Compliance

For transactions where Whop is required by law to collect and remit indirect tax—such as qualifying marketplace transactions where Whop is acting as a marketplace facilitator, deemed supplier, or merchant of record—Whop has engaged Numeral to:

  • Monitor registration thresholds by jurisdiction;
  • Classify transactions based on Whop's role and the applicable legal framework;
  • Apply jurisdiction-specific taxability rules based on product or service type;
  • Calculate applicable indirect taxes at the point of sale; and
  • Support collection, filing, and remittance obligations in applicable jurisdictions.

In these scenarios, Numeral functions as part of Whop's internal compliance and control environment.

Seller-Managed Tax Compliance

For transactions where indirect tax responsibility remains with the seller, Whop supports seller-managed compliance through Numeral account integrations. Sellers may elect to connect their own Numeral accounts to Whop in order to:

  • Monitor registration requirements;
  • Apply seller-specific taxability determinations and product mappings;
  • Calculate, collect, and remit applicable indirect taxes directly; and
  • Maintain jurisdiction-specific filings and documentation under their own accounts.

In seller-managed configurations, Numeral operates as the seller's tax compliance platform, and Whop does not assume indirect tax liability beyond any obligations imposed by applicable law.

Hybrid and Seller-Elected Models

Whop's operating model also supports hybrid configurations in which sellers elect to delegate indirect tax collection and remittance to Whop, even where Whop is not required by law to do so. In such cases, Whop may act as merchant of record or deemed supplier depending on the transaction structure and jurisdictional requirements.

Numeral supports these configurations by applying the appropriate role-based logic, transaction classification, and reporting to ensure that indirect tax is collected, remitted, and documented consistently with the applicable legal framework and contractual arrangements.

Role-Based Determination and Controls

Across all configurations, Numeral applies a role-based determination framework that distinguishes between:

  • Transactions for which Whop bears indirect tax responsibility; and
  • Transactions for which sellers retain indirect tax responsibility.

This framework is supported by documented logic, system controls, and advisor-reviewed configurations.

6. Ongoing Review and Change Management

Whop's indirect tax policy and operating model are designed to reflect current interpretations of applicable marketplace facilitator, deemed supplier, and indirect tax laws across jurisdictions. Certain aspects of Whop's indirect tax treatment, including jurisdiction-specific applications of these rules, seller-elected tax handling models, and the legal characterization of Whop's role in particular transaction flows, will remain subject to ongoing review.

Whop is working with Deloitte Tax LLP to evaluate these matters and refine its indirect tax framework as necessary to reflect evolving regulatory guidance, jurisdictional interpretations, and business operations. As these reviews are completed, Whop may update its tax registrations, transaction classifications, collection practices, or contractual arrangements on a prospective basis to ensure continued compliance with applicable law.

This policy reflects Whop's current operating framework and is intended to provide transparency into its indirect tax approach. It does not constitute a representation that Whop's tax treatment will remain unchanged in all jurisdictions or transaction scenarios, nor does it limit Whop's ability to modify its compliance approach in response to legal, regulatory, or business developments.
